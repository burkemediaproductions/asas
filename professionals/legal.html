Legal & estate contexts

Guidance for Legal Professionals

Recognizing relational dynamics that may influence estate planning, guardianship, power of attorney and vulnerable-adult decisions.

ASAS is not a legal or clinical diagnosis. It is a descriptive framework intended to support professional awareness without assigning blame or evaluating any specific family.

Potential dynamics in legal settings

  • One sibling controlling access to the parent or client
  • Conflicting narratives about family history or another sibling
  • Sudden changes in estate plans, beneficiaries or powers of attorney
  • A vulnerable adult expressing fear, confusion or divided loyalties
  • A sibling excluded from updates or discouraged from participating
  • Pressure to expedite decisions without balanced communication
  • Attempts to restrict communication between counsel and other family members

Protecting autonomy

Legal professionals can safeguard autonomy by verifying wishes directly with the client whenever possible, documenting conversations carefully, maintaining clear boundaries, and staying alert to possible undue influence or restricted access.

Professional neutrality

The goal is not to identify an “alienator.” It is to preserve the integrity of the legal process and ensure the vulnerable adult’s actual wishes remain central.

Use direct verification when the stakes are high.

Relational conflict can distort information. Independent professional judgment remains essential.
