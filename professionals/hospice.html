Family care settings

Guidance for Hospice & Medical Professionals

Keep the patient’s voice central when complex sibling dynamics enter medical and end-of-life care.

ASAS is not a clinical diagnosis. This page offers a descriptive framework for awareness, not an evaluation of any person or family.

Potential dynamics in care settings

  • One sibling speaking for the patient or controlling access
  • Conflicting stories about family members
  • Sudden changes in who is “allowed” to visit or communicate
  • A patient expressing fear, confusion or divided loyalties
  • A sibling isolated or excluded from updates

Supporting the vulnerable patient

When appropriate, verify wishes directly, document communication consistently, maintain clarity around medical facts, offer neutral emotional support, and recognize when one person’s narrative may not represent the full picture.

Neutrality with observation

Care teams do not need to intervene in family conflict to keep the patient’s autonomy and emotional safety at the center.

Clinical judgment and hospice protocols always come first.

ASAS education is supplemental context, never a replacement for professional standards.
