Insight

The Coalition Around the Alienator

Alienation can be reinforced by a network of active participants and passive failures.

Alienation is often more than a story about one destructive relationship. It is also a story about who repeats a narrative, who avoids discomfort, who asks questions and who stays present.

Roles that can reinforce alienation

Conflict-avoiders may side with the loudest or closest person. Authority figures may unintentionally lend credibility to a one-sided narrative. “Echoes” repeat claims without verification. Beneficiaries may have incentives to stay silent. Others simply disappear when presence matters most.

The counterweight

Witnesses, steady friends, advisors, truth-seekers, protectors and emotionally grounded supporters can help a targeted family member remain connected to reality and identity outside the conflict.

The central lesson

Healthy systems reward verification, direct communication and moral courage over convenience and social pressure.

This article describes general relational roles. It does not evaluate or accuse any specific person or community.
