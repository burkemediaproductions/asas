Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about adult sibling alienation, aging parents, family risk and the ASAS framework.

What is Adult Sibling Alienation Syndrome (ASAS)?+

ASAS is an educational framework describing patterns in which adult sibling relationships are damaged through behaviors such as manipulation, misinformation, exclusion, gatekeeping and emotional pressure, often during a parent’s decline. It is not a clinical diagnosis.

Is ASAS the same as parental alienation?+

No. Parental alienation usually refers to a child being influenced against a parent. ASAS focuses on adult siblings and the family dynamics around aging parents or other vulnerable relatives.

What are common warning signs?+

Commonly reported signs include sudden access restrictions, information blackouts, distorted narratives, one sibling controlling communication or care, and pressure on relatives to align with one side.

Does ASAS always involve malicious intent?+

No. Fear, insecurity, caregiving stress or misguided attempts to protect a parent can also contribute. The framework focuses on observable patterns and impact rather than assuming intent.

Can ASAS affect grandchildren or extended family?+

Yes. Loyalty tests, narrative sharing and exclusion can spread across generations and involve cousins, in-laws, grandchildren, friends and professionals.

How can families protect aging parents?+

Transparent caregiving plans, clear legal documents, direct communication, regular contact from multiple people, and involvement of neutral qualified professionals can reduce vulnerability.

What documentation can be useful?+

Simple factual timelines, written communications, denied-access records, copies of relevant legal or medical documents, and notes from professional conversations can help establish clarity.

Is ASAS legally recognized?+

ASAS itself is not a legal term. Some behaviors discussed within the framework may overlap with established legal or clinical concerns such as undue influence, elder abuse, coercive control, exploitation or capacity issues. Consult a qualified professional for legal questions.
