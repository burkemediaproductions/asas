ASAS Glossary

Definitions for terms used throughout the ASAS educational framework.

Alienator

The sibling who initiates, drives, or sustains the alienation process. The alienator manipulates family perceptions, controls access to vulnerable parents, and shapes narratives that isolate targeted siblings. This term describes behavior, not a diagnosis.

Boundary Distortion

A pattern in which personal, emotional, or relational boundaries are ignored, violated, or manipulated to maintain control or influence within the family system.

Caregiving Monopoly

A situation in which one sibling positions themselves as the sole or primary caregiver to a vulnerable parent, using this role to control access, information, and decision‑making. The monopoly often becomes a tool for dominance rather than genuine care.

Crisis Manipulation

The creation, exaggeration, or exploitation of a crisis to justify control, restrict access, or escalate tension. Common during medical events, emergencies, or transitions in care.

Emotional Coercion

The use of guilt, fear, obligation, or emotional pressure to influence a parent or sibling’s decisions. Often framed as “concern” or “responsibility,” but functions as control.

Enablers

Individuals who—intentionally or unintentionally—support the alienator’s narrative or behaviors. Enablers may be misled, intimidated, or unaware of the broader pattern.

Estrangement

A prolonged or permanent breakdown in contact or communication between siblings or between a parent and child. Estrangement is often the end result of sustained alienation behaviors.

Family Image Management

The alienator’s effort to present themselves publicly as the “good child,” “protector,” or “responsible one,” while privately engaging in divisive or harmful behaviors.

Family System Collapse

The breakdown of healthy communication, trust, and connection within a family due to sustained alienation behaviors.

False Narrative

A distorted or fabricated storyline used to justify alienation behaviors. False narratives often evolve over time and become increasingly dramatic or emotionally charged.

Gatekeeping

Controlling access to a vulnerable parent, information, or family events. Gatekeeping may include restricting visits, withholding updates, or creating emotional or logistical barriers, willfully sabotaging scheduled visits.

Henchmen / Pawns

Individuals who adopt or repeat the alienator’s narrative, often without full understanding of the situation. They may participate in exclusion or spread misinformation.

Identity Erosion

The gradual wearing down of the targeted sibling’s sense of self through repeated mischaracterization, exclusion, or narrative distortion. Over time, the targeted sibling may be portrayed as incompetent, dangerous, irresponsible, or uncaring—regardless of reality.

Information Blackout

A deliberate withholding of updates, decisions, or communication about a vulnerable parent or related family matters. Information blackouts isolate targeted siblings and reinforce the alienator’s control.

Isolation Pattern

A recurring set of behaviors that gradually separate the targeted sibling from parents, family gatherings, information, or decision‑making.

Narrative Control

The alienator’s effort to shape how events, relationships, and conflicts are understood by others. This may involve rewriting history, omitting key facts, or exaggerating or fabricating grievances.

Psychological Projection

A defense mechanism in which the alienator attributes their own negative traits, motives, or behaviors to the targeted sibling.

Reconciliation Barrier

Any emotional, logistical, or narrative obstacle that prevents healing or reconnection. These barriers may be intentionally maintained or may arise from long‑term damage. Mental health struggles and deficiencies can create impossible barriers. An alienator's character flaws, such as envy, jealousy, malice or greed can also be impediments to healing or reconnection.

Sibling‑Driven Alienation

A pattern in which one sibling systematically undermines another sibling’s relationship with parents or family through exclusion, misinformation, or emotional coercion.

Smear Tactic

A deliberate attempt to discredit the targeted sibling through exaggerated claims, misrepresentation, or false narratives.

Targeted Sibling

The sibling who becomes the focus of the alienator’s efforts. Targeted siblings are often subjected to exclusion, misinformation, or character attacks. The targeted sibling is typically the one or the ones that are blocking the alienator's goals, which are generally greed and power driven.

Triangulation

A manipulation tactic in which the alienator uses a third party—often a parent or another sibling—to relay messages, escalate conflict, or reinforce division.

Vulnerable Parent

An aging or dependent parent who becomes susceptible to influence, manipulation, or isolation due to declining health, cognitive changes, or exclusive reliance on one child for everything.

Whisper Campaign

A covert pattern of negative storytelling, insinuation, or rumor‑spreading designed to damage the targeted sibling’s reputation or credibility. This campaign is often implemented long before the targeted sibling or siblings are even aware of a conflict.

Personality Traits & Patterns Commonly Seen in Alienating Behavior

These traits are behavioral patterns, not diagnoses. They describe tendencies frequently observed in individuals who drive alienation dynamics.

Cluster B Personality Patterns

A group of behavioral tendencies characterized by emotional volatility, interpersonal instability, entitlement, and a strong need for control. These patterns may include dramatic behavior, hypersensitivity to criticism, and difficulty maintaining long term stable relationships.

Control Needs

A persistent drive to dominate decisions, narratives, and relationships. Control needs often intensify when the alienator feels threatened, insecure, or challenged.

Covert Narcissism

A form of narcissistic behavior marked by hypersensitivity, resentment, quiet superiority, and victim‑posturing. Covert narcissists often appear self‑effacing or fragile while privately harboring entitlement and envy.

Grandiose Narcissism

A more visible form of narcissistic behavior characterized by arrogance, self‑importance, attention‑seeking, and a belief in one’s superiority. Grandiose narcissists often dominate conversations and demand admiration.

Lack of Empathy

Difficulty recognizing or caring about the emotional needs of others. This trait allows alienators to justify exclusion, manipulation, or cruelty without experiencing guilt.

Paranoia (Non‑clinical)

A tendency toward suspiciousness, assuming negative motives, or believing others are plotting against them. In family systems, this may manifest as fear that siblings will “take advantage,” “interfere,” or “ruin things.”

Self‑Importance

A belief that one’s opinions, decisions, or role in the family are uniquely correct or superior. Self‑importance often fuels gatekeeping and narrative control.

Borderline Personality Traits (Non‑diagnostic)

Patterns include emotional instability, fear of abandonment, black‑and‑white thinking, and intense reactions to perceived slights. These traits can amplify conflict and drive divisive behavior.
