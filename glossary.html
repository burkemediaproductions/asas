ASAS Glossary

Definitions for recurring adult sibling alienation concepts.

These definitions describe relational behaviors and patterns. They are not clinical diagnoses and should not be used to label specific individuals without appropriate professional evaluation.

Alienator

The sibling who initiates, drives or sustains alienation behaviors. The term describes behavior, not a diagnosis.

Caregiving Monopoly

A situation in which one sibling positions themselves as the sole or primary caregiver and controls access, information or decisions.

Crisis Manipulation

Creation, exaggeration or exploitation of a crisis to justify control, restrict access or increase tension.

Emotional Coercion

Use of guilt, fear, obligation or emotional pressure to influence decisions.

Enablers

People who intentionally or unintentionally support the alienator’s narrative or behaviors.

Estrangement

A prolonged or permanent breakdown in contact or communication.

Gatekeeping

Controlling access to a vulnerable parent, information or family events.

Identity Erosion

Gradual wearing down of a targeted sibling’s identity through repeated mischaracterization and exclusion.

Information Blackout

Deliberate withholding of updates, decisions or communication about a vulnerable parent or family matter.

Narrative Control

Efforts to shape how events, relationships and conflict are understood by others.

Targeted Sibling

A sibling who becomes the focus of exclusion, misinformation, character attacks or access restrictions.

Triangulation

Using a third party to relay messages, escalate conflict or reinforce division.

Vulnerable Parent

An aging or dependent parent who may be more susceptible to influence, isolation or control due to health, cognitive or practical dependency.

Whisper Campaign

Covert negative storytelling, insinuation or rumor-spreading designed to damage credibility.
