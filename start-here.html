First orientation

Adult sibling alienation rarely begins with one dramatic event.

It often grows through control, isolation, selective information and subtle changes in access until a new family reality has formed.

1

1. Observe

Notice access restrictions, information blackouts, sudden narrative shifts, loyalty tests or unilateral decisions.

Explore →2

2. Verify

Whenever appropriate, seek direct communication and independent verification rather than relying on one person’s account.

Explore →3

3. Document

Keep simple factual records of major access changes, care decisions, legal documents and significant communications.

4. Get qualified help

Legal, medical, elder-care or mental-health professionals can evaluate circumstances this website cannot.

A useful distinction

Patterns matter more than one disagreement.

A conflict between siblings, even a painful one, is not automatically ASAS. The framework is most useful when multiple behaviors form a recurring system: access control, narrative dominance, information restriction, triangulation, coercion, isolation or exploitation of vulnerability.

Look for repetition, escalation, timing around parental decline or care transitions, and whether one sibling is becoming the sole source of access, information or decision-making.

Important

This site educates. It does not diagnose.

If you are facing abuse, coercion, financial exploitation, medical risk, immediate safety concerns or legal conflict, consult a qualified professional or appropriate authority who can assess the actual facts.
