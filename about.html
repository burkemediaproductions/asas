Adult Sibling Alienation Syndrome

Adult Sibling Alienation Syndrome (ASAS) is an educational framework developed to help families, professionals, and researchers understand relational patterns that lead to estrangement between adult siblings.

ASAS is not a clinical diagnosis. It is a descriptive model identifying how undue influence, narrative-shaping, communication breakdowns, gatekeeping and vulnerability can fracture sibling relationships and destabilize family systems, often placing aging parents at risk.

Why this framework exists

Many people experiencing sibling alienation struggle to find language for what is happening. ASAS provides terminology, explanations and general guidance to illuminate patterns and reduce confusion without making clinical claims about any specific individual.

Not every family system can be repaired through improved communication. In some entrenched situations, the priority may shift from reconciliation toward protection, distance, transparency and outside professional help.

Core principles

  • Describe behavior, not diagnosis
  • Keep vulnerable adults at the center
  • Encourage direct verification
  • Promote transparency and documentation
  • Separate education from personal advice

Darkness and illumination

The visual language mirrors the mission.

Grayscale imagery reflects grief, isolation, confusion and family rupture. Yellow represents illumination, warning, hope and the possibility of a new beginning through knowledge.
